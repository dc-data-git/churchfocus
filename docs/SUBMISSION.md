# Submission description (250 words max)

ChurchFocus helps people find nearby churches through an ongoing conversation and public-source research. It is designed for individuals and for helpers, such as campus pastors and nonprofit workers, who otherwise repeat hours of website reading for each person.

People describe what matters in their own words. The app records preferences and reasons in an append-only memory log that the person can review and correct. It uses a mapped Christian vocabulary lexicon to interpret familiar church language, without introducing belief topics the person has not raised.

A starting place triggers a background search. Nearby churches appear with fit labels, distance, and an adjustable radius. Explicit denomination exclusions filter results; practice preferences affect fit. When asked, it reads up to five chosen churches’ websites in parallel, with visible progress, and summarizes service times, public staff and active ministries with source quotes.

People can ask factual questions about a church. Answers require verified source quotes; uncertainties become editable Open questions. A tool-using deep-research agent investigates each church broadly, with sermons central, and works through those questions. Its report includes source links, stated versus observed practice, and questions for a visit.

A knowledge base of 217 US religious groups provides denomination context through an MCP interface. Sensitive priors require human review. The app avoids congregant, donor, minor and payment data and never contacts churches. Research is logged, and incomplete findings remain explicit.

Built for the 2026 Gloo AI Hackathon by a professor and three Tabor College students.
